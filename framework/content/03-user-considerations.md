# User considerations
intro: Now the person who will use it. Quality is not the whole story: fit, support, and watchfulness matter just as much.

## Does the person using it know what could go wrong?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Good. Knowing the risks is what lets someone notice them early.
### If answered: Not sure
Walk it through together: what it does with information, what it cannot do, and what it is not a replacement for.
### If answered: No
Go through the basics before it gets used. Someone who does not know the risks cannot watch for them.

## If something goes wrong, does the person know where to turn?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Good. Knowing the way back is part of using anything safely.
### If answered: Not sure
Work it out together before it is needed: who to tell if something feels off, and how to reach them.
### If answered: No
Make a small plan now: one person to tell, and one way to reach them. That is the difference between a problem and a dead end.

## Does the person's age or situation call for extra care right now?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Match the support to it: check in more often, and sooner. Age and hard seasons both change what a technology should be asked to carry.
### If answered: Not sure
When in doubt, lean toward more support. It is easier to step back later than to catch up after a problem.

## Will an adult be nearby or checking in?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Good. Agree on what checking in looks like: regular enough to matter, light enough that it does not push the person to hide their use.
### If answered: Not sure
Settle this before it matters: who checks in, and how often.
### If answered: No
Ask whether that changes the choice. For a younger or more vulnerable person, a nearby adult is often what turns a tool into a support.

## How much of the person's day does it take up?
- A little
- A fair amount
- A lot
- Not sure
### If answered: A fair amount
Worth watching. Check that it is not quietly crowding out other things.
### If answered: A lot
A lot of time with any technology pushes other things out: sleep, people, getting outside. Look at what it is displacing.
### If answered: Not sure
Keep an eye on it for a week. Amounts are easier to see in hindsight than in the moment.

## Guidance
Fit matters as much as quality. The same technology can be a good choice for one person and a poor one for another, depending on age, situation, and the support around them. Where something gives you pause, trust the pause. Raise the support, or step back, and talk it over with someone you trust.
