# Quality of technology
intro: Look at what stands behind the technology: who made it, what supports it, and how it treats the people who use it.

## Is it clear who made this technology, and why?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Not sure
Spend a few minutes looking into who is behind it — an about page, the company name, a news search — before going further.
### If answered: No
Treat its claims with extra caution. When it is unclear who is behind a product, there is no one to hold accountable if something goes wrong.

## Is there evidence that it works?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Look at what the evidence covers, and who it was tested with. Results for one group do not always carry over to others.
### If answered: Not sure
Look for research, a public evaluation, or a study behind the claims. If you cannot find one, treat it as unproven for now.
### If answered: No
If nothing backs up its claims, its promises may not hold up in practice. Weigh that before trusting it with someone's mental health.

## If someone needs urgent help while using it, does the technology show them where to get it?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Check that the path to help is easy to find — reachable from the main screens, not buried in a corner.
### If answered: Not sure
Open it and picture a hard moment: where would someone go? If you cannot find a path, assume there is not one.
### If answered: No
People may turn to it when they are struggling. Without a clear path to real help, it should not be anyone's only source of support.

## Does it explain, in plain language, what happens to what you share?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Check the explanation against what the app actually asks for. See whether it can be used without sharing more than you want to.
### If answered: Not sure
Look for a privacy notice you can read and understand. If it is missing or hard to find, that is worth weighing on its own.
### If answered: No
If it does not say what happens to your information, assume it collects and shares more than you would expect.

## Does it use design features meant to keep people coming back?
- Yes
- Not sure
- No
### If answered: Yes
Streaks, constant reminders, and rewards for returning are built to hold attention. Ask whether that pull fits the person using it — and how it would feel to set it down.
### If answered: Not sure
Watch for the usual signals: notifications, streak counters, rewards for checking in. If you spot any of them, weigh it as a yes for now.

## For a chatbot: when it cannot help, does a real person take over?
types: chatbot
- Yes
- No
### If answered: Yes
Proceed with caution: a human safeguard is a good sign, but keep working through the other questions.
### If answered: No
If you cannot tell whether a person takes over, treat it as a no for now. Without a person in the loop, a chatbot cannot catch it when things go wrong — consider an MHCC-certified chatbot instead.

## Guidance
Look at the pattern across your answers, not any single one. A technology with clear accountability, real evidence, and a safety net is easier to trust. Where the picture is mixed, decide which gaps matter most in your situation — and what would need to change before you would rely on it.
